# Proposed Vaulter knowledge model

Proposed domain language for reviewing knowledge in Vaulter. These definitions extend the existing Vaulter vocabulary for discussion; they have not been adopted as application contracts.

## Knowledge and sources

**Subject**:
The person, topic or other matter that knowledge and questions concern. A subject can have several accounts and descriptions without becoming several subjects.

**Topic**:
A broad subject chosen to organize a recurring area of inquiry or activity. Narrower subjects and their material can belong under a topic without becoming topics themselves.

**Topic membership**:
An editorial association of a subject or its material with a topic. Membership does not assert a factual relationship between the subjects.

**Claim**:
An assertion that can be reviewed against retained source material. It may be expressed in prose or by a structured record.
_Avoid_: Fact when support or truth has not been established.

**Evidence**:
Retained material considered when assessing a claim. Being considered as evidence does not establish that it supports the claim.

**Source document**:
An independently retained document or excerpt with its origin and attribution preserved. Capturing a document preserves what it said, not whether everything it said is true.

**Citation**:
A connection between a selected statement and a supporting source passage, retaining the recorded content on which that association was reviewed. Its supporting passage belongs to underlying source material rather than another Wiki page.

**Challenge**:
An attributed connection between a claim and source material considered to contradict it. It retains the disagreement rather than deciding the claim's truth automatically.

**Review**:
An attributed assessment of a particular claim and the material considered for it. Its conclusion applies to the assertion that was reviewed.

**Interpretation**:
An attributed reading of recorded evidence, such as a summary, inferred event or decision. Later corrections coexist with the original interpretation.

## Relationships and time

**Relationship**:
A named connection between subjects or other retained records. When it asserts knowledge, its meaning includes any stated qualification and the time it describes.

**Modality**:
Whether an assertion describes an actual state, an expectation, an intention or a hypothetical situation. It is distinct from how certain the speaker is.

**Certainty**:
The degree of assurance expressed about an assertion, attributed to the person or interpretation making it. It is distinct from whether a reference supports that assertion.

**Effective time**:
The period or time about which a claim makes its assertion. It is distinct from when the assertion was reported or recorded.

## Activity and choices

**Event**:
An occurrence or scheduled occurrence concerning subjects, with its timing and reported status retained.

**Decision**:
A choice adopted by an actor, retaining the direction chosen and any stated reason. A recommendation or wish is not an adopted decision.

**Task**:
A stated commitment to take an action, retaining who is responsible and any stated due time.

**Outcome**:
A retained account of what happened to an activity or commitment. It can establish a later state without changing what was originally intended.

**Correction**:
A recorded revision of an earlier account or interpretation. The earlier account remains available rather than being presented as something never said.

**Supersession**:
A later choice or direction replacing an earlier one. It does not mean the earlier choice was never made.
